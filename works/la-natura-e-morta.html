la natura è morta (there is still life)

Thousands of cargo airplanes fly over our heads every day. We're talking about the smallest part of a huge logistics network that provides food and supplies all around the world; it is an invisible, gigantic network that leaves permanent traces on ecological and social balances in order to meet a growing demand. Just like airplanes, flies glide over food and land on whatever seems most appealing to them. They devour the food slowly, making it look disgusting, fueling the idea that they are a symptom of illness, filth and decay.

In this work, they play the main role of breaking the silence of the scene with their buzzing, taking over the food and making the best of it, inviting us to learn from them how to adapt instead of trying to control and capitalize on entire ecosystems.
